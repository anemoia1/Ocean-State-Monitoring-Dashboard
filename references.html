References & Methodology

Data provenance, scientific references, image credits, ethical statement, and course attribution for the OceanPulse platform.

Primary Data Sources

NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information (NCEI)

Extended Reconstructed Sea Surface Temperature v5 (ERSSTv5); World Ocean Atlas 2023 (salinity, temperature, dissolved oxygen); Global Ocean Heat and Salt Content; Marine Heatwave monitoring products.

ncei.noaa.gov

NASA Physical Oceanography DAAC (PO.DAAC)

Multiscale Ultra-high Resolution Sea Surface Temperature (MUR-SST) product; GRACE-FO land ice mass balance; TOPEX/Poseidon, Jason-1/2/3, and Sentinel-6 satellite altimetry for sea level monitoring.

podaac.jpl.nasa.gov

Argo International Program

Global array of 3,800+ profiling floats providing real-time temperature and salinity profiles from the surface to 2000 m depth in the global ocean. Ocean heat content anomaly estimates derived from Argo gridded products (Roemmich–Gilson Argo Climatology).

argo.ucsd.edu

IPCC Sixth Assessment Report (AR6) — 2021

Working Group I: The Physical Science Basis. Chapters 2 (Changing State of the Climate System), 3 (Human Influence on the Climate System), and 9 (Ocean, Cryosphere and Sea Level Change). Sea level rise projections, SST trends, and ocean acidification scenarios referenced throughout.

ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1

ESA Climate Change Initiative (CCI) — Ocean Colour

Global chlorophyll-a concentration products from merged multi-sensor satellite ocean colour data (SeaWiFS, MODIS, MERIS, OLCI). Used for phytoplankton biomass and primary productivity assessments.

climate.esa.int

Global Ocean Observing System (GOOS) / IOCCP

Surface ocean CO₂ atlas (SOCAT v2023) and Surface Ocean pCO₂ and pH products used for ocean acidification monitoring. Dissolved oxygen data from the GO-SHIP hydrographic survey programme.

goosocean.org

Ocean Biodiversity Information System (OBIS)

Species occurrence data and range shift analysis for marine biodiversity stress quantification. Coral bleaching event data from the NOAA Coral Reef Watch programme and the Global Coral Bleaching Database.

obis.org

Ethical Statement

OceanPulse is committed to open science and transparent communication of ocean monitoring data. All numerical values, trend analyses, and projections presented on this platform are grounded in peer-reviewed science and primary observational data from authoritative international programmes. Where data have been synthesised, simplified, or estimated for visualisation purposes, this is clearly indicated.


The Ocean Stress Index (OSI) is an original conceptual metric developed for illustrative and educational purposes. Its weighting coefficients and sub-index formulations have not undergone peer review and should not be used for operational decision-making without further validation.


We acknowledge the efforts of the thousands of scientists, ship crews, and technicians globally who maintain the observational infrastructure that makes ocean monitoring possible. We support equitable access to ocean data and endorse the FAIR data principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable).
